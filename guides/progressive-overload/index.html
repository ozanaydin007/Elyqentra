Progressive Overload: The Rule Behind Every Program That Works

Your body adapts to the demands placed on it. To keep improving, those demands have to increase over time. That idea is progressive overload.

Ways to progress

Add weight to the bar. Do more reps with the same weight. Add a set. Improve technique and range of motion. Shorten rest periods for conditioning goals. Any of these increases the challenge.

Double progression

Pick a rep range such as 8-12. Start with a weight you can lift for 8 reps on all sets. Each session, try to add reps. When you hit 12 on every set, increase the weight and start back at 8. It is simple and it works for almost every exercise.

How close to failure?

For muscle growth, most working sets should end with about 0-3 reps left in the tank. Going to absolute failure on every set of heavy compound lifts adds a lot of fatigue for little extra benefit.

Track your training

Write down the weight, sets and reps of every exercise. Without a log, it is very hard to know whether you are actually progressing. That is exactly what the workout tracker on this site is built for.

When progress stalls

Check sleep and food first. Then check whether you are training hard enough. If both are fine, change the rep range, swap an exercise variation, or take a deload week.

Frequently asked questions

How fast should I add weight?

Beginners can often add weight every session on big lifts. Intermediate lifters progress weekly or monthly instead.

Can I overload with bodyweight exercises?

Yes: add reps, slow the tempo, add a pause, or move to a harder variation.
