Chest/Biceps, Back/Triceps, Legs/Shoulders workout split

Pair chest with biceps and back with triceps, then a legs and shoulders day, on a rolling four-day cycle.

  • Training days4 per week
  • LevelIntermediate
  • Best forArm-focused hypertrophy with antagonist pairing
  • Cycle length4 days

This split pairs a big muscle with a small muscle that it does not depend on. Biceps are fresh on chest day, and triceps are fresh on back day, so arms get high-quality work twice per cycle.

It runs on a rolling cycle of three training days and one rest day, so the training days move around the calendar from week to week.

Who this split is for

This split is a good fit for intermediate lifters who want bigger arms, like shorter sessions, and do not mind training days moving around the calendar. It is also popular with people who find arms get neglected in push/pull splits.

Why it works

Pairing chest with biceps and back with triceps means each arm muscle is trained after a session that did not fatigue it. Biceps are fresh on chest day because pressing barely uses them; triceps are fresh on back day for the same reason. Arms get higher-quality sets than in a push/pull layout.

The rolling four-day cycle trains each muscle about 1.75 times per week, close to the twice-weekly frequency research favors, with only three training days in every four.

The workouts

Rest 2-3 minutes between heavy compound sets and 60-90 seconds on isolation work. Leave 1-3 reps in reserve on most sets, and add weight or reps over time.

Weekly sets per muscle

Hard sets each muscle gets per week on this split. A set counts fully for the main muscle an exercise trains and as half a set for helper muscles; cycles that are not exactly seven days long are averaged per week. Most people build muscle well with roughly 10-20 weekly sets per muscle; strength and conditioning programs are intentionally lower for some muscles.

How to progress

  1. Use double progression on all exercises.
  2. Give the main chest and back lifts priority and progress them first; arm work follows.
  3. Every fifth or sixth cycle, deload by halving the sets.
  4. Add volume to arms only if they lag: one set at a time, up to about 12-14 weekly sets.

How to adapt it

Prefer fixed weekdays?

Run it Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday with legs and shoulders on Saturday, repeating chest and biceps the next Monday. Frequency drops slightly.

Shoulders lagging?

Add 3 sets of lateral raises to back and triceps day.

Legs need more?

Split legs and shoulders into two separate days and run a five-day cycle.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Arms trained fresh, not pre-fatigued
  • Rolling cycle trains each muscle about 1.75 times a week
  • Short, focused sessions

Cons

  • Training days shift around the week
  • Shoulders and legs share a demanding day

What happens if you miss a day?

In the Elyqentra tracker, a missed session moves to the next day and the rest of the 4-day cycle follows. Nothing is lost; your week simply extends by a day.

Common mistakes

Rushing the leg and shoulder day

It is the most demanding day in the cycle. Start with legs and keep shoulder volume moderate.

Overdoing arm volume

Arms recover quickly but tendons do not. Large jumps in arm volume can cause elbow pain.

Ignoring the rolling schedule

The cycle only works if rest days fall every fourth day. Do not stack extra sessions in a row.

Frequently asked questions

Why train chest with biceps instead of triceps?

Triceps are already fatigued by pressing. Pairing chest with biceps means both muscles can be trained fresh.

Is this split good for beginners?

Beginners usually do better with full-body training, but it can work once the main lifts are learned.
