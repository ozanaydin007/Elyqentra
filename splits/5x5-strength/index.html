5x5 Strength workout split

Two alternating barbell workouts of five sets of five, three days a week.

  • Training days3 per week
  • LevelBeginner
  • Best forRaw strength on the big barbell lifts
  • Cycle length14 days

Classic 5x5 programs alternate two short workouts built on the squat, bench press, overhead press, barbell row and deadlift. You add a little weight every session for as long as you can.

It is one of the fastest ways for a beginner to get strong. Once linear progress stalls, move to Upper / Lower or PHUL.

Who this split is for

5x5 Strength is designed for beginners who want to get strong on the barbell lifts as quickly as possible, and for people returning to lifting after a long break. It is not intended as a long-term program for advanced lifters.

Why it works

Five sets of five reps is heavy enough to build strength quickly and light enough to practice technique on every set. Beginners respond so well to training that they can add weight almost every session for several months.

Squatting every session builds leg strength fast, while alternating bench press and overhead press keeps the shoulders balanced. The short exercise list means sessions are quick and progress is easy to measure.

The workouts

Rest 2-3 minutes between heavy compound sets and 60-90 seconds on isolation work. Leave 1-3 reps in reserve on most sets, and add weight or reps over time.

Weekly sets per muscle

Hard sets each muscle gets per week on this split. A set counts fully for the main muscle an exercise trains and as half a set for helper muscles; cycles that are not exactly seven days long are averaged per week. Most people build muscle well with roughly 10-20 weekly sets per muscle; strength and conditioning programs are intentionally lower for some muscles.

How to progress

  1. Start with weights that feel easy, often just the empty bar for presses, and add 2.5 kg (5 lb) to squats, bench, overhead press and rows each session, and 5 kg (10 lb) to deadlifts.
  2. If you fail to complete 5x5 at the same weight for three sessions in a row, reduce that lift by about 10% and build back up.
  3. When resets happen repeatedly, switch to weekly progression or move to PHUL or Upper / Lower.
  4. Most people run linear progression successfully for three to six months.

How to adapt it

Want bigger arms?

Add 2-3 sets of curls and dips or triceps extensions at the end of each session.

Deadlift feels too heavy for one set?

Do 3 sets of 5 instead of 1 set.

Low back fatigue?

Replace barbell rows with chest-supported rows.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Simple and measurable
  • Fast strength gains for beginners
  • Short sessions

Cons

  • Little direct arm or shoulder isolation
  • Progress stalls after a few months

What happens if you miss a day?

In the Elyqentra tracker, a missed session moves to the next day and the rest of the 14-day cycle follows. Nothing is lost; your week simply extends by a day.

Common mistakes

Starting too heavy

Starting near your limit ends linear progress within weeks. Start light and let the weekly increases do the work.

Ignoring sleep and food

Adding weight every session requires recovery. Linear progress stalls early in a calorie deficit.

Skipping the deload

Grinding the same failed weight repeatedly wastes weeks.

Frequently asked questions

Is 5x5 good for muscle growth?

It builds muscle well for beginners, but the low volume for arms and shoulders means accessory work helps.

How long can I run 5x5?

Usually three to six months, until resets happen frequently.
