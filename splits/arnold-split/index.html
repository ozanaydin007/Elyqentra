Arnold Split workout split

Chest and back together, shoulders and arms together, then legs, each twice a week.

  • Training days6 per week
  • LevelAdvanced
  • Best forHigh-volume mass building for experienced lifters
  • Cycle length7 days

Made famous by golden-era bodybuilders, this split pairs chest with back so you can superset opposing muscles, then trains shoulders with arms, then legs. The cycle runs twice per week.

It is one of the most demanding mainstream splits. Use it when you already recover well from four or five days of training and want more volume.

Who this split is for

The Arnold Split is for advanced lifters with excellent recovery habits who want very high volume and can train six days a week. It suits people in a calorie surplus far better than people dieting.

Why it works

Supersetting chest and back means one muscle rests while its opposite works. That saves time and lets you perform a lot of high-quality volume in one session.

With each muscle trained twice a week and supersets packing in extra work, weekly volume for chest and back goes well beyond 20 sets, as the table below shows. That is above what most people need, which is why this program is for advanced lifters and why recovery matters so much.

The workouts

Rest 2-3 minutes between heavy compound sets and 60-90 seconds on isolation work. Leave 1-3 reps in reserve on most sets, and add weight or reps over time.

Weekly sets per muscle

Hard sets each muscle gets per week on this split. A set counts fully for the main muscle an exercise trains and as half a set for helper muscles; cycles that are not exactly seven days long are averaged per week. Most people build muscle well with roughly 10-20 weekly sets per muscle; strength and conditioning programs are intentionally lower for some muscles.

How to progress

  1. Start with about 70% of the listed sets for the first two weeks and build up.
  2. Use double progression; with supersets, progress each exercise separately.
  3. Deload every fourth or fifth week rather than every sixth, because fatigue accumulates quickly.
  4. If performance drops two weeks in a row, cut volume by about a third.

How to adapt it

Only 3 days a week?

Run the three workouts once a week. Volume per session stays high while weekly frequency falls.

Recovery struggling?

Remove the last exercise of each day before cutting anything else.

No partner for supersets?

Perform supersets with exercises that use the same station, such as a cable fly and cable row.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Very high weekly volume
  • Antagonist supersets save time
  • Each muscle trained twice a week

Cons

  • Six hard days per week
  • Needs excellent sleep and nutrition to recover
  • Not suitable for beginners

What happens if you miss a day?

In the Elyqentra tracker, a missed session moves to the next day and the rest of the 7-day cycle follows. Nothing is lost; your week simply extends by a day.

Common mistakes

Starting at full volume

Jumping straight into six high-volume days is a common route to burnout.

Running it during a cut

The volume is hard to recover from in a calorie deficit. Choose Upper / Lower or PPL instead.

Sacrificing form in supersets

Moving fast between exercises should not mean sloppy reps.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Arnold Split good for natural lifters?

It can work for experienced natural lifters with good recovery, but many get similar results with less volume.

How long are Arnold Split workouts?

Usually 75-90 minutes.
