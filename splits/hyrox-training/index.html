HYROX Training workout split

Running, station work and compromised running to prepare for a HYROX race.

  • Training days5 per week
  • LevelIntermediate to advanced
  • Best forPreparing for a HYROX fitness race
  • Cycle length7 days

A HYROX race alternates 1 km runs with eight workout stations: SkiErg, sled push, sled pull, burpee broad jumps, rowing, farmers carry, sandbag lunges and wall balls. Training has to build both running and the ability to run on tired legs.

This plan mixes strength days, run sessions and compromised running, where you run straight after station work.

Who this split is for

HYROX Training is for people preparing for a HYROX race, whether a first finish or a faster time, and for anyone who wants a demanding mix of running and functional strength. It requires access to a gym with sleds, a rower and a SkiErg, or suitable substitutes.

Why it works

A HYROX race is eight 1 km runs, each followed by a workout station. Roughly half of total race time is spent running, so running fitness is the biggest single factor in performance.

The other key skill is running well on tired legs after sleds, lunges and wall balls. Compromised running sessions practice exactly that, while strength days build the leg and grip strength the stations demand.

The workouts

Rest 2-3 minutes between heavy compound sets and 60-90 seconds on isolation work. Leave 1-3 reps in reserve on most sets, and add weight or reps over time.

Weekly sets per muscle

A set counts fully for the main muscle an exercise trains and as half a set for helper muscles; cycles that are not exactly seven days long are averaged per week. Most people build muscle well with roughly 10-20 weekly sets per muscle; strength and conditioning programs are intentionally lower for some muscles.

How to progress

  1. Build weekly running volume gradually, by no more than about 10% a week.
  2. Every two weeks, extend the compromised running session by one more run-and-station pair.
  3. Increase sled loads or distances gradually toward race weights for your division.
  4. Three to four weeks before the race, do a near-full simulation; in the final week, cut volume by about half.

How to adapt it

No sled available?

Use heavy prowler-style pushes on turf, hill sprints or heavy farmer's carries as substitutes.

No SkiErg?

Use rowing or banded straight-arm pulldowns combined with fast step-ups.

New to running?

Start with run-walk intervals and build to continuous 1 km repeats over several weeks.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Race-specific preparation
  • Builds work capacity and endurance
  • Strength is maintained

Cons

  • Needs access to sleds, rower and SkiErg
  • High overall training load

What happens if you miss a day?

In the Elyqentra tracker, a missed session moves to the next day and the rest of the 7-day cycle follows. Nothing is lost; your week simply extends by a day.

Common mistakes

Neglecting running

Many athletes spend too much time on stations. Running volume decides most of the race.

Going out too fast

Starting the first 1 km at sprint pace costs far more time later. Practice even pacing.

Never practicing wall balls fatigued

Wall balls come last in the race. Train them when you are already tired.

Frequently asked questions

How long should I train for a HYROX race?

Most people need 8-16 weeks of focused preparation, depending on their starting fitness.

Do I need to lift heavy for HYROX?

Moderate strength is enough. Sleds and lunges reward strong legs, but endurance matters more.
