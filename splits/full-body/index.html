Full Body workout split

Train the whole body in every session, three non-consecutive days per week.

  • Training days3 per week
  • LevelBeginner
  • Best forBeginners, busy schedules and fat loss
  • Cycle length7 days

A full-body program hits every major muscle group each session. With three sessions a week, each muscle gets three exposures, which is ideal for learning the main lifts quickly.

Because there is a rest day between every session, full-body training is forgiving: missing one day costs little, and it pairs well with a calorie deficit.

Who this split is for

Full Body training is ideal for beginners, people returning after a break, anyone with an unpredictable schedule, and people dieting who want strength training without high fatigue. It also suits busy professionals who can only commit to three sessions.

Why it works

Training each muscle three times a week gives beginners frequent practice on the main lifts. Skill improves fastest when movements are repeated often, and early strength gains come largely from learning the lifts.

Because volume is spread across three sessions, no single workout destroys any one muscle. That keeps soreness manageable and lets beginners recover between sessions even while learning to train hard.

The workouts

Rest 2-3 minutes between heavy compound sets and 60-90 seconds on isolation work. Leave 1-3 reps in reserve on most sets, and add weight or reps over time.

Weekly sets per muscle

Hard sets each muscle gets per week on this split. A set counts fully for the main muscle an exercise trains and as half a set for helper muscles; cycles that are not exactly seven days long are averaged per week. Most people build muscle well with roughly 10-20 weekly sets per muscle; strength and conditioning programs are intentionally lower for some muscles.

How to progress

  1. Beginners can often add weight every session on squats, deadlifts and presses for the first weeks.
  2. When a lift stalls, switch that exercise to double progression in a 6-10 rep range.
  3. After 8-12 weeks, add a fourth set to the first exercise of each session if recovery is good.
  4. When all main lifts progress only weekly instead of every session, consider moving to Upper / Lower.

How to adapt it

Only 2 days a week?

Run Full Body A and B and add one set to each main lift. Two sessions a week still build meaningful strength and muscle.

Want to add cardio?

Put walks or easy cycling on rest days, and keep intense cardio away from the day before leg-heavy sessions.

Training at home?

Use goblet squats, dumbbell Romanian deadlifts, push-ups, dumbbell rows and pull-ups on a bar.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Only three gym days
  • High practice frequency for main lifts
  • Very forgiving of missed sessions

Cons

  • Long sessions if volume grows
  • Harder to add lots of isolation work

What happens if you miss a day?

In the Elyqentra tracker, a missed session moves to the next day and the rest of the 7-day cycle follows. Nothing is lost; your week simply extends by a day.

Common mistakes

Adding too many exercises

Full-body sessions get long quickly. Stick to five or six exercises and push them hard.

Training on consecutive days

Two full-body sessions back to back mean training tired muscles. Keep a rest day between sessions.

Staying on beginner weights

The program only works if loads increase. Log every session.

Frequently asked questions

Can I build muscle with full body three times a week?

Yes. For beginners and early intermediates it builds muscle as well as most split programs.

How long should a full-body workout be?

About 45-70 minutes, including warm-ups.
