PHUL (Power Hypertrophy Upper Lower) workout split

Two heavy power days and two higher-rep hypertrophy days on an upper/lower structure.

  • Training days4 per week
  • LevelIntermediate
  • Best forGetting stronger and bigger at the same time
  • Cycle length7 days

PHUL combines strength and size work. The first two days of the week use heavy compound lifts in low rep ranges. The last two use moderate weights, higher reps and more isolation exercises.

It suits lifters who want to see their main lifts climb while still building visible muscle.

Who this split is for

PHUL suits intermediate lifters who care about both strength and size, want a four-day schedule and are comfortable with heavy compound lifts. It is a natural next step after a beginner strength program such as 5x5.

Why it works

Heavy loads in the 3-5 rep range drive strength gains most effectively, while moderate loads with more total sets add muscle efficiently. PHUL places both in the same week, so each type of training supports the other.

Research comparing heavy and lighter loads found similar muscle growth when sets were close to failure, but more strength with heavier loads. PHUL takes advantage of both findings.

The workouts

Rest 2-3 minutes between heavy compound sets and 60-90 seconds on isolation work. Leave 1-3 reps in reserve on most sets, and add weight or reps over time.

Weekly sets per muscle

Hard sets each muscle gets per week on this split. A set counts fully for the main muscle an exercise trains and as half a set for helper muscles; cycles that are not exactly seven days long are averaged per week. Most people build muscle well with roughly 10-20 weekly sets per muscle; strength and conditioning programs are intentionally lower for some muscles.

How to progress

  1. On power days, add weight to the main lift when you complete all sets at the top of the rep range.
  2. On hypertrophy days, use double progression in the 8-12 range.
  3. Every five or six weeks, deload by reducing weights about 10% and sets by half.
  4. Test a heavy single or triple on your main lifts after each deload if you want to track strength.

How to adapt it

Want more arm work?

Add 2 sets of curls and triceps extensions on both upper days.

Short on time?

Superset the accessory movements on hypertrophy days.

Training for powerlifting?

Keep the power days as they are and replace some isolation work with paused or close-grip variations of the main lifts.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Builds strength and size together
  • Only four days
  • Clear structure

Cons

  • Heavy days need good technique
  • Less isolation work than a body-part split

What happens if you miss a day?

In the Elyqentra tracker, a missed session moves to the next day and the rest of the 7-day cycle follows. Nothing is lost; your week simply extends by a day.

Common mistakes

Grinding heavy reps with poor form

Power days are for crisp, controlled heavy reps. Missed reps and form breakdown should be rare.

Treating hypertrophy days as easy

Moderate weights still need to be taken close to failure.

Testing maxes too often

Constant max testing adds fatigue and injury risk without building strength.

Frequently asked questions

PHUL or Upper / Lower?

They are similar. PHUL has a stronger split between heavy and moderate days, which suits people who want visible strength progress.

Can women do PHUL?

Yes. The principles are the same for everyone.
