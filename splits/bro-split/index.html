Bro Split workout split

One major muscle group per day: chest, back, shoulders, arms and legs.

  • Training days5 per week
  • LevelIntermediate
  • Best forBodybuilding-style volume, one muscle group per day
  • Cycle length7 days

The classic bodybuilding split gives each muscle group its own day. Sessions are focused and allow a lot of volume for one area, with a full week of recovery before it is trained again.

The main drawback is frequency: each muscle is trained once a week. Many lifters still grow well on it, especially when sessions are hard and close to failure.

Who this split is for

The Bro Split suits intermediate lifters who enjoy bodybuilding-style training, like a full session devoted to a single muscle, and can train five days a week. It is less suitable for beginners and for people whose weekly schedule changes often.

Why it works

Each session concentrates a lot of volume on one area, which allows a variety of angles and exercises and produces a strong pump. Every muscle then gets a full week to recover.

In a study comparing a body-part split with a total-body routine of the same weekly volume in trained men, the higher-frequency total-body routine produced somewhat more growth in some muscles. The Bro Split still works, but it relies on very hard sessions to make up for low frequency.

Weekly sets per muscle

Hard sets each muscle gets per week on this split. A set counts fully for the main muscle an exercise trains and as half a set for helper muscles; cycles that are not exactly seven days long are averaged per week. Most people build muscle well with roughly 10-20 weekly sets per muscle; strength and conditioning programs are intentionally lower for some muscles.

How to progress

  1. Track the first two exercises of each day closely and use double progression on them.
  2. Because each muscle is trained only once a week, push sets close to failure: 0-2 reps in reserve on most work.
  3. Add volume carefully. More than about 10-12 hard sets for one muscle in a single session adds fatigue faster than results.
  4. Deload every 6-8 weeks.

How to adapt it

Want more frequency?

Add 3-4 sets of chest and back work on shoulder and arm days, or switch to the Arnold Split.

Only 4 days a week?

Merge arms into chest and back days: chest and triceps, back and biceps, shoulders, legs.

Missed a day?

In the Elyqentra tracker the missed session moves forward, so no muscle goes untrained for two weeks.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Very focused, pump-heavy sessions
  • Lots of variety per muscle
  • Easy to follow

Cons

  • Each muscle only once a week
  • A missed day means a muscle goes untrained for two weeks

What happens if you miss a day?

In the Elyqentra tracker, a missed session moves to the next day and the rest of the 7-day cycle follows. Nothing is lost; your week simply extends by a day.

Common mistakes

Junk volume

Doing eight chest exercises at low effort. Fewer, harder sets work better.

Skipping leg day

Leg day is often the first to go. Place it early in the week if that helps you keep it.

Training arms right after back or chest

Arms are already tired from compound lifts, so arm day the day after chest or back can feel weak. Keep at least one day in between.

Frequently asked questions

Is the bro split outdated?

Not if it is trained hard and consistently. Research suggests higher frequency is slightly better, but many lifters build excellent physiques with body-part splits.

How many exercises per muscle?

Usually four or five, totaling about 10-16 hard sets.
