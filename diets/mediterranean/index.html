Mediterranean Diet

Olive oil, fish, vegetables and legumes: one of the most studied healthy diets.

The Mediterranean pattern is based on vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, fish and olive oil, with moderate dairy and little red meat.

It is consistently linked to good heart health and is easy to adapt to a training lifestyle by adding a bit more protein.

Who this diet is for

The Mediterranean diet suits people who want a heart-healthy, flexible way of eating with a lot of variety. It works especially well for long-term health, maintenance and gradual fat loss.

Why it works

In a large randomized trial, people following a Mediterranean diet supplemented with extra virgin olive oil or nuts had fewer major cardiovascular events than those on a reduced-fat control diet.

Its foods are naturally rich in fiber, unsaturated fats and polyphenols. With a little extra protein, it fits a training lifestyle easily.

What to eat

  • Extra virgin olive oil
  • Fish two or more times a week
  • Legumes, vegetables and whole grains
  • Yogurt, feta, nuts and fruit

What to limit

  • Processed meat
  • Refined sugar

How to start

  1. Make extra virgin olive oil your main cooking and dressing fat.
  2. Eat fish or seafood at least twice a week and legumes at least three times a week.
  3. Base meals on vegetables, whole grains such as bulgur, and legumes.
  4. Use yogurt, feta and eggs to raise protein toward about 1.6 g per kg.
  5. Keep red and processed meat occasional, and choose fruit and nuts for snacks.

Common mistakes

Pouring olive oil without measuring

Olive oil has about 120 calories per tablespoon. During a cut, measure it.

Too little protein for lifters

Traditional Mediterranean meals can be modest in protein. Add fish, yogurt, eggs or legumes.

Treating wine as essential

Wine is often linked to the pattern, but you gain the benefits without it.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Mediterranean diet good for weight loss?

Yes, when it is combined with a calorie deficit. Its high fiber content helps with fullness.

Can I build muscle on a Mediterranean diet?

Yes. Increase protein portions and eat a small calorie surplus.
