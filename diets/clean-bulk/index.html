Clean Bulk

A modest calorie surplus from mostly whole foods to build muscle while limiting fat gain.

A clean bulk, also called a lean bulk, puts you about 250-350 calories above maintenance. That is enough to support muscle growth without the rapid fat gain of a large surplus.

Aim to gain roughly 0.25-0.5% of your bodyweight per week. If the scale climbs faster than that for two weeks in a row, take 100-150 calories off.

Who this diet is for

A clean bulk suits anyone who wants to add muscle and is already reasonably lean: roughly under 15-18% body fat for men and under 25-28% for women. It is the default bulking approach for intermediate lifters, who gain muscle more slowly and therefore need only a small surplus.

Why it works

Muscle growth is slow. Even a well-trained beginner rarely gains more than about 0.5-1 kg of lean tissue a month, and an intermediate lifter gains far less. Any surplus beyond what the body can use for building muscle is stored mostly as fat.

Research comparing smaller and larger surpluses in trained athletes found that the larger surplus led to more fat gain without a meaningful increase in muscle. That is the core argument for keeping the surplus modest.

Calories and macros

CaloriesAbout 300 calories above maintenance per day.
Protein1.8 g per kg of bodyweight (0.82 g per lb)
Fat25% of calories
CarbohydratesThe remaining calories after protein and fat

What to eat

  • Lean meats, fish and eggs
  • Rice, oats, potatoes and whole-grain bread
  • Fruit and vegetables at most meals
  • Olive oil, nuts and nut butters

What to limit

  • Fried food and pastries
  • Sugary drinks
  • Alcohol

How to start

  1. Find your maintenance calories with the calorie calculator and add 250-350.
  2. Set protein at 1.6-2.2 g per kg of bodyweight and fat at 20-30% of calories; carbohydrates fill the rest.
  3. Weigh yourself 3-7 mornings a week and track the weekly average.
  4. Aim for 0.25-0.5% bodyweight gain per week. Adjust by 100-150 calories if you are outside that range for two weeks.
  5. Measure your waist every two weeks. A rapidly growing waist with little strength gain means the surplus is too big.

Common mistakes

Using clean as a reason to undereat

Some people choose only low-calorie whole foods and end up at maintenance. Calorie-dense whole foods such as oats, rice, nut butters, olive oil and whole milk make the target easier.

Judging progress day to day

Water and food volume can move the scale 1-2 kg overnight. Only the weekly trend is meaningful.

Bulking without progressive training

Extra food only turns into muscle if the training keeps getting harder. Track your lifts as closely as your weight.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a clean bulk last?

Usually 4-9 months, as long as you stay within your chosen body fat limit and your lifts keep improving.

Can I eat junk food on a clean bulk?

Yes, in moderation. A common rule is roughly 80-90% whole foods and the rest flexible.
