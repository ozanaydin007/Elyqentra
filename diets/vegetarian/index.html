Vegetarian Diet

Meat-free eating with dairy and eggs, built to hit protein targets for training.

A vegetarian diet removes meat and fish but keeps eggs and dairy, which makes hitting protein targets straightforward.

Build meals around eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, tofu, tempeh, lentils and beans.

Who this diet is for

A vegetarian diet suits people who avoid meat and fish for ethical, environmental, religious or health reasons but still eat eggs and dairy. It works well for every training goal.

Why it works

Eggs and dairy provide complete, high-quality protein, so hitting protein targets is straightforward. Greek yogurt, skyr and cottage cheese are among the most protein-dense foods per calorie.

Professional nutrition organizations consider well-planned vegetarian diets healthful and suitable for athletes at all stages of life.

What to eat

  • Eggs, Greek yogurt and cottage cheese
  • Tofu, tempeh and seitan
  • Lentils, chickpeas and beans
  • Whole grains, nuts and seeds

What to limit

  • Meals built only on cheese and pasta
  • Low-protein days

How to start

  1. List your main protein sources: eggs, Greek yogurt, skyr, cottage cheese, tofu, tempeh, seitan, lentils and beans.
  2. Include at least one high-protein item in every meal to reach about 1.6-2.0 g per kg.
  3. Pair iron-rich plant foods such as lentils and spinach with vitamin C foods to improve absorption.
  4. Include omega-3 sources such as walnuts, chia and flaxseed, or consider an algae-based supplement.
  5. Consider creatine: vegetarians usually have lower muscle creatine and often respond well to it.

Common mistakes

Replacing meat with pasta and cheese

Swapping meat for refined carbs and cheese raises calories and fat without adding enough protein.

Ignoring iron

Vegetarians, especially women, are at higher risk of low iron. A blood test can tell you whether to supplement.

Relying on beans alone for protein

Legumes are healthy but provide fewer grams of protein per calorie than dairy, eggs or soy.

Frequently asked questions

Can vegetarians build as much muscle as meat eaters?

With enough total protein and good training, yes. Dairy and eggs make this especially easy.

Do I need protein powder as a vegetarian?

Not necessarily, but whey or casein can help on busy days.
