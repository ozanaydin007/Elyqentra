Intermittent Fasting 16:8

Eat all daily food within an 8-hour window, then fast for 16 hours.

16:8 fasting does not change what you eat, only when. Many people use it because two larger meals feel more satisfying than several small ones.

Results come from total calories and protein, not the fasting window itself. Place the window around training if you can. Fasting is not recommended during pregnancy or for people with a history of disordered eating.

Who this diet is for

16:8 intermittent fasting suits people who are not hungry in the morning, prefer fewer and larger meals, or find a clear eating window easier than counting every snack. It is not recommended during pregnancy, for people with diabetes on glucose-lowering medication without medical guidance, or for anyone with a history of disordered eating.

Why it works

Studies in resistance-trained people found that 16:8 time-restricted eating led to similar or slightly better fat loss than a normal schedule, while strength was maintained, largely because people ate a little less.

A larger randomized trial of 16:8 eating without calorie counting found only small weight loss, similar to the control group. The eating window is a tool to help control calories, not a separate mechanism for fat loss.

What to eat

  • Protein-rich first meal
  • Plenty of vegetables and fiber
  • Water, black coffee and tea during the fast

What to limit

  • Overeating junk food once the window opens

How to start

  1. Choose a window that fits your day, for example 12:00-20:00.
  2. Make your first meal high in protein; aim for about 40-50 g.
  3. Fit your full protein target into two or three meals.
  4. If you train in the morning while fasted, eat a protein-rich meal soon after.
  5. Drink water, black coffee or plain tea during the fast; try a shorter 14-hour fast first if 16 hours feels hard.

Common mistakes

Overeating in the window

Fasting does not cancel calories. Large, ultra-processed meals can easily erase the deficit.

Too little protein

Fewer meals make it harder to reach protein targets. Plan protein-heavy meals.

Forcing it when it does not fit

If fasting causes binge eating, poor sleep or weak training, a normal meal schedule is a better choice.

Frequently asked questions

Does 16:8 burn more fat?

Not on its own. It helps some people eat less, which is what leads to fat loss.

Will fasting make me lose muscle?

Not if you eat enough protein in your eating window and keep lifting.
