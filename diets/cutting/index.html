Cutting (Definition)

A moderate calorie deficit with high protein to lose fat and keep muscle.

Cutting, often called getting defined, means eating below maintenance so the body uses stored fat. A deficit of about 500 calories a day typically leads to around 0.5 kg (1 lb) of loss per week.

Protein is set high because it protects muscle in a deficit and keeps you full. Keep lifting heavy: the training signal is what tells your body to hold on to muscle. Avoid extreme deficits; they cost muscle, energy and mood.

Who this diet is for

Cutting is for anyone who wants to lose body fat while keeping as much muscle and strength as possible: lifters finishing a bulk, people preparing for an event, or anyone carrying more fat than they want.

Why it works

Fat loss requires a sustained energy deficit. The challenge is that the body can also break down muscle for energy, especially when the deficit is large, protein is low or training stops.

A study of athletes losing weight found that a slower rate of about 0.7% of bodyweight per week preserved lean mass and strength better than a faster rate of about 1.4%. High protein and continued heavy lifting are the two main muscle-protecting levers.

Calories and macros

CaloriesAbout 500 calories below maintenance per day.
Protein2.2 g per kg of bodyweight (1.0 g per lb)
Fat25% of calories
CarbohydratesThe remaining calories after protein and fat

What to eat

  • Lean protein at every meal
  • High-volume vegetables
  • Potatoes, oats and fruit for carbs
  • Measured portions of fats

What to limit

  • Liquid calories
  • Mindless snacking
  • Calorie-dense sauces

How to start

  1. Subtract 300-500 calories from maintenance; larger people can use the upper end.
  2. Set protein at 2.0-2.4 g per kg of bodyweight.
  3. Keep fat at or above about 20% of calories and fill the rest with carbohydrates, placing more of them around training.
  4. Keep lifting with the same weights; reduce sets slightly if recovery suffers, but do not switch to light weights.
  5. Aim to lose 0.5-1% of bodyweight per week. Take a week at maintenance every 6-10 weeks on long cuts if energy drops.

Common mistakes

Cutting calories too far

Very large deficits speed up muscle loss, increase hunger and usually lead to rebound eating.

Doing endless cardio instead of lifting

Cardio helps burn calories, but lifting is what tells the body to keep muscle.

Forgetting about daily steps

Non-exercise activity often drops unnoticed during a diet. Keeping steps steady protects the deficit.

Frequently asked questions

Why has my weight stopped dropping?

Water retention can hide fat loss for 1-2 weeks. If the weekly average has not moved for 2-3 weeks, reduce calories by about 100-200 or add steps.

Should I do refeed days?

They are optional. Some people find a planned higher-carb day helps training and adherence, but total weekly calories still decide fat loss.
