Keto Diet

Very low carbohydrate, high fat eating that shifts the body into ketosis.

The ketogenic diet keeps net carbohydrates around 20-30 g per day, which pushes the body to produce ketones from fat. Many people find it reduces appetite.

Fat loss on keto still comes from a calorie deficit. High-intensity training performance may drop, especially in the first weeks. People with diabetes, kidney issues or who take medication should talk to a doctor before starting.

Who this diet is for

Keto can suit people who prefer high-fat foods, find that low-carb eating controls their appetite, or enjoy structured rules. It is less suited to athletes who rely on high-intensity or high-volume training, since carbohydrates fuel that kind of work.

Why it works

Keeping carbohydrates very low lowers insulin and pushes the liver to produce ketones from fat. Many people report reduced hunger, which can make a calorie deficit easier.

Controlled studies have found that when calories and protein are matched, ketogenic and higher-carb diets lead to similar fat loss. Keto is a tool for controlling appetite, not a metabolic shortcut.

Calories and macros

CaloriesUses the calorie target from your goal: surplus to gain, deficit to lose, maintenance to hold.
Protein1.8 g per kg of bodyweight (0.82 g per lb)
FatThe remaining calories after protein and carbs
CarbohydratesUnder about 25 g net carbs per day

What to eat

  • Meat, fish and eggs
  • Low-carb vegetables like spinach, broccoli and zucchini
  • Avocado, olive oil, butter and nuts
  • Cheese and full-fat yogurt in moderation

What to limit

  • Bread, rice, pasta and potatoes
  • Sugar and most fruit
  • Beer and sweet drinks

How to start

  1. Keep net carbohydrates around 20-30 g per day; net carbs are total carbs minus fiber.
  2. Set protein at about 1.6-2.0 g per kg of bodyweight; eating enough protein does not stop ketosis for most people.
  3. Fill the rest of your calories with fat from foods such as eggs, fatty fish, olive oil, avocado, nuts and cheese.
  4. Add salt to food and drink plenty of water. Early fatigue, headaches and cramps, often called keto flu, are usually linked to fluid and electrolyte loss.
  5. Expect a 1-3 kg drop in the first week. It is mostly water and stored carbohydrate, not fat.

Common mistakes

Assuming fat is unlimited

Fat loss on keto still depends on a calorie deficit. Butter, oils and cheese add up quickly.

Too few vegetables

Low-carb vegetables provide fiber, potassium and magnesium. Leaving them out often causes constipation.

Expecting the same high-rep performance

Strength usually holds up, but high-volume sets and intervals may feel harder, especially in the first few weeks.

Frequently asked questions

Can I build muscle on keto?

Yes, with adequate protein, enough total calories and progressive training, although some studies suggest gains may be slightly slower than with more carbohydrate.

Who should avoid keto?

People with type 1 diabetes, kidney or liver disease, a history of pancreatitis or disordered eating, and those who are pregnant or on certain medications should talk to a doctor first.
