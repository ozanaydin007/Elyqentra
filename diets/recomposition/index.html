Body Recomposition

A small deficit with very high protein to lose fat and gain muscle at the same time.

Recomposition works best for beginners, people returning after a break and those carrying more body fat. A small deficit plus hard training and high protein can shift body composition while the scale barely moves.

Track progress with measurements, photos and strength, not just bodyweight.

Who this diet is for

Recomposition works best for beginners, people returning after a long break, and people with moderate body fat. It is also useful for anyone who wants to look leaner without seeing big changes on the scale.

Why it works

When training is new or returning, the body responds strongly to resistance exercise. Combined with high protein, this allows muscle gain even while a small deficit draws on fat stores.

A review of recomposition research concluded that trained lifters can also build muscle and lose fat at the same time, though more slowly, when protein is high and training is well designed.

Calories and macros

CaloriesAbout 200 calories below maintenance per day.
Protein2.2 g per kg of bodyweight (1.0 g per lb)
Fat27% of calories
CarbohydratesThe remaining calories after protein and fat

What to eat

  • High-protein foods at every meal
  • Carbs around training
  • Plenty of vegetables

What to limit

  • Alcohol
  • Highly processed snacks

How to start

  1. Eat about 0-300 calories below maintenance.
  2. Set protein high: 2.0-2.4 g per kg of bodyweight.
  3. Train every muscle at least twice a week with progressive overload.
  4. Track progress with waist measurements, monthly photos and strength, not just bodyweight.
  5. Give it at least 8-12 weeks before judging results.

Common mistakes

Judging only by the scale

Weight may barely change during a successful recomposition. Measurements and strength tell the real story.

Using recomposition when a cut is needed

People with a lot of body fat usually get faster, more motivating results from a proper cut first.

Inconsistent training

Recomposition depends on a strong training signal. Missed weeks slow it down more than in other phases.

Frequently asked questions

How long does body recomposition take?

Visible changes usually appear after 2-3 months of consistent training and eating.

Is recomposition possible for advanced lifters?

It is possible but slow. Advanced lifters usually make faster progress with separate bulking and cutting phases.
