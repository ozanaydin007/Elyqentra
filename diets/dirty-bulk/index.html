Dirty Bulk

A large calorie surplus with flexible food choices to gain size and weight as fast as possible.

A dirty bulk prioritizes hitting a big calorie target over food quality. It works for hardgainers who struggle to eat enough, but it also adds fat quickly.

A sensible version still hits the protein target every day and keeps at least half the diet as whole foods. Keep a close eye on waist measurement, and switch to a clean bulk if it rises quickly.

Who this diet is for

A dirty bulk is mainly for true hardgainers: lean people with a fast metabolism or small appetite who struggle to gain weight even when they try. It can also suit athletes in sports where extra bodyweight matters more than leanness. For most other people a clean bulk works better.

Why it works

The main obstacle for hardgainers is eating enough. Calorie-dense, palatable foods make a large surplus achievable when whole foods alone leave someone too full.

The cost is fat gain. Muscle can only be built so fast, so a large surplus adds noticeably more fat, which later has to be removed with a longer cut.

Calories and macros

CaloriesAbout 700 calories above maintenance per day.
Protein1.8 g per kg of bodyweight (0.82 g per lb)
Fat32% of calories
CarbohydratesThe remaining calories after protein and fat

What to eat

  • Whole milk and calorie-dense smoothies
  • Pasta, rice, bagels and granola
  • Red meat, eggs and cheese
  • Nut butters, trail mix and dried fruit

What to limit

  • Days below your protein target
  • Relying only on fast food
  • Large amounts of alcohol

How to start

  1. Start at about 500-700 calories above maintenance rather than jumping higher.
  2. Hit protein first: at least 1.6 g per kg every day, however flexible the rest of the diet is.
  3. Use liquid calories such as milk and smoothies; they fill you up far less than solid food.
  4. Set a clear stopping point, for example a waist measurement or body fat level, before you start.
  5. Keep at least half of your food from whole sources to cover fiber and micronutrients.

Common mistakes

Chasing the scale with no limit

Gaining 1 kg a week feels productive, but most of it is fat and water. Even on a dirty bulk, 0.5-1% of bodyweight per week is plenty.

Letting protein slip

Fast food is calorie-rich but often protein-poor. Low protein turns a bulk into simple fat gain.

Ignoring health markers

Months of very processed food can affect blood lipids, energy and digestion. Keep vegetables and fruit in the diet.

Frequently asked questions

Is a dirty bulk unhealthy?

A short, controlled dirty bulk in a healthy young person is unlikely to cause harm, but long periods of excess processed food and fat gain are not good for health.

Will I lose the fat easily later?

It has to be removed with a calorie deficit, which can take several months if a lot was gained.
