Vegan Diet

Fully plant-based eating planned for complete protein and key nutrients.

A vegan diet excludes all animal products. Muscle can be built on it with good planning: combine several protein sources through the day and aim slightly higher on total protein.

Vitamin B12 must come from fortified foods or a supplement. Vitamin D, iodine, omega-3, iron and zinc are also worth checking with a professional.

Who this diet is for

A vegan diet is for people who avoid all animal products. It can support muscle gain, fat loss and endurance performance, but it requires more planning around protein and a few key nutrients.

Why it works

A randomized study found that young men on a high-protein vegan diet supplemented with soy protein gained muscle as well as omnivores eating a similar amount of protein from whey. Total protein and training mattered more than the source.

Plant proteins are often lower in leucine and some essential amino acids, so aiming slightly higher on total protein and mixing sources across the day makes up the difference.

What to eat

  • Tofu, tempeh, seitan and edamame
  • Lentils, beans and chickpeas
  • Pea or soy protein powder
  • Whole grains, nuts and seeds

What to limit

  • Relying on processed vegan junk food
  • Single-source protein days

How to start

  1. Aim for about 1.8-2.2 g of protein per kg, from tofu, tempeh, seitan, edamame, lentils, beans and pea or soy protein.
  2. Combine legumes and grains across the day for a full amino acid profile.
  3. Take vitamin B12 from a supplement or fortified foods; it is not reliably available from plants.
  4. Check vitamin D, iodine, iron, zinc, calcium and omega-3 (EPA/DHA), using fortified foods or algae oil where needed.
  5. Consider creatine, since vegan diets contain almost none.

Common mistakes

Too little total protein

Plant foods are bulkier and less protein-dense. Without planning, intake often falls well below training targets.

Skipping B12

B12 deficiency develops slowly but can cause serious problems. Supplementing is the standard recommendation.

Relying on processed vegan products

Many meat and cheese substitutes are high in fat and low in protein. Read the label.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best vegan protein powder?

Soy and pea-rice blends have good amino acid profiles. Choose third-party tested products.

Is soy bad for testosterone?

Research on normal dietary amounts of soy has not shown meaningful effects on testosterone in men.
